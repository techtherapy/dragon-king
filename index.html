Introduction · 緣起

A hidden jewel of the canon

在浩瀚的佛典中，有一部鮮少人注意卻極為殊勝的經典——《佛說海龍王經》。記載了釋迦牟尼佛在靈鷲山為海龍王及眷屬宣說大乘深義，並應邀至龍宮弘法的莊嚴場景。

Among the vast collection of Buddhist scriptures there is a text seldom noticed yet exceedingly sublime — the Sutra Spoken by the Buddha on the Sea Dragon King. It records the solemn scene in which Sakyamuni Buddha, upon Vulture Peak, expounded the profound meaning of the Mahayana for the Sea Dragon King and his retinue, and accepted their invitation to propagate the Dharma in the Dragon Palace.

龍

Origins of the Dragon Clan

龍族的來歷

Dragon kings are not merely Dharma-protecting deities but sentient beings capable of attaining Buddhahood — cultivators of former lives, reborn as dragons, still turning within the Six Realms like every people of the world.

The Three Burning Sufferings

三熱苦

Long-lived and vast in form, dragons still endure three sufferings: scorching sand that burns to the marrow, humiliation of form, and the constant dread of the Garuda. Even splendour is not beyond suffering.

Benefits of Recitation · 誦持功德

Why recite this sutra

Upholding the Sutra of the Sea Dragon King is said to bring the greatest of blessings, for it moves the beings who dwell nearest to our world.

  • 四部護持

    The four classes — Devas, Dragons, Asuras and Garudas — extend their protection and bestow blessings on those who recite and circulate the sutra.

  • 福報增長

    Merit and worldly wellbeing increase; those who have printed and shared this sutra have swiftly seen a spiritual response in daily life.

  • 障礙消除

    Obstacles dissolve as the Eight Classes are harmonised — the sutra soothes the unseen world that surrounds the human realm.

  • 明心見性

    Beyond blessings, the sutra opens the cultivator to the true meaning of realising the mind and seeing one's original nature.

From the Writings · 文集

Wishes in the Dragon King Treasure Vase Practice龍王寶瓶的祈願

“Grandmaster, when you cast the Dragon King Treasure Vase, what do you pray for?”

In answering a young disciple's question, His Holiness draws out the deeper meaning of the practice — fulfil wishes, give freely, want nothing, become a Buddha — and the reason desire is allowed to lead the way at all.
