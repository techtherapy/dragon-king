Chapter 011 *Blessings in a Dream*
from book #35 *Revelation of Reincarnation*

*by Lu Sheng-yen*

There was a man named Cheng Quan, in his thirties, who came seeking to change his occupation and also to ask about his blessings.

“Are my blessings many?”

I replied, “Many.”

“Are my blessings few?”

I replied, “Few.”

Cheng Quan found my answers rather strange, and he asked,

“First you say many, then you say few. Why is that?”

I answered,

“You gain and you lose; you lose and you gain. Naturally, sometimes they are many and sometimes they are few.”

Cheng Quan smiled.

“For how long are they many?”

“One night.”

“For how long are they few?”

“One night.”

“And what is the reason for this one night?” Cheng Quan was again utterly bewildered.

“It is nothing but a dream!” I replied.

Hearing my answer, Cheng Quan found it deeply strange. He thought I was perhaps an eccentric man, or that I had simply answered offhandedly. Yet on reflection, he felt there might be some special hidden mystery within it, perhaps some philosophy or revelation.

So Cheng Quan added one further question:

“Is there a hidden meaning in it?”

I replied,

“You will have the answer very soon.”

Cheng Quan asked no more. As he was about to leave, I called him back: “When you have the answer, be sure to come and see me again!”

He nodded.

After Cheng Quan returned home, that very night he had a dream. He dreamed —

that he could fly, as though wings had grown from his body. He flew above the sea, and though he watched rosy clouds veiling the heavens and waves rising one after another, he felt no fear.

As he flew, he saw in the distance, amid the shimmering water and mist, tier upon tier of pavilions appear. It was a great metropolis.

The metropolis bustled with people coming and going. One of them glimpsed Cheng Quan and was greatly astonished. He at once called out to the crowd, and they all cheered thunderously:

“The King has come!”

Cheng Quan cried out, “I am not the King.”

“You are the King. The King wears upon his chest a golden plaque bearing the character 王 (‘King’).”

Cheng Quan looked, and indeed a golden plaque bearing the character 王 (‘King’) hung upon his chest. He did not know when it had come to be there.

The crowd enthroned Cheng Quan as their King and escorted him into the royal palace. The palace was resplendent and magnificent, with vermilion carved beams and glazed roof tiles. Its four walls were crystalline and bright, casting dazzling reflections, and the tower in which he dwelt rose to meet the Milky Way. Within the palace were heaped rare and marvelous treasures whose radiance dazzled the eyes, every one of them beyond price.

Before long, palace maids and attendants came forward, supporting the Queen as she emerged. The Queen was like a celestial maiden, radiating splendor, her jade pendants ringing softly as she moved.

The King and Queen held a banquet for the ministers. All that was served were delicacies of the mountains and seas, rarely found in this world, and wine and roasted meats were passed round several times.

When the wine was done, palace maids led the way into the bedchamber. There stood a bed of coral, richly adorned with the Eight Treasures and the Eight Auspicious Symbols. From the tassels outside the curtains hung luminous pearls, glittering like starlight, and the quilts and bedding were fragrant and soft.

For that one night, all the wealth, rank, and glory of the human world were his; warm jade and fragrant softness filled his arms!

Cheng Quan suddenly recalled Lu Sheng-yen’s words — “Are my blessings many?” “Many.” Cheng Quan smiled. Indeed they were many.

When Cheng Quan awoke, he found himself lying on his own wooden plank bed. The royal palace, the rare treasures, the palace maids and the Queen had vanished in an instant. Everything he had possessed was gone.

He was struck dumb.

He touched his own head. This had been no ordinary dream; everything had been utterly clear. The royal splendor and its jewels seemed still before his eyes, enviable. The Queen’s beauty had left a deep impression, and even the intimacy they had shared was in no way vague. What could it all mean?

Yet once he awoke from the dream, all of it was gone. Again Cheng Quan recalled Lu Sheng-yen’s words — “Are my blessings few?” “Few.”

Gained, and lost!

It had indeed been “one night.”

Cheng Quan was a man of considerable roots of wisdom. He contemplated this dream with great care, and at the same time turned my words over and over in his mind.

There arose in him an urgent longing to devote himself to the search for Truth, and a faint aversion toward the pursuit of worldly blessings. He pondered deeply the question of “gain and loss”: from ancient times until now, who has truly gained? And who has truly lost?

Cheng Quan had long grown weary of his present way of life. He could not endure the rigid routine of a clock-bound career. What meaning was there in living like this until old age? And yet only in this way could he support himself and his family.

He thought of Lu Sheng-yen. Perhaps Lu Sheng-yen could guide him into a life simple and plain, cut off from all luxury, wholly free from the competition and vanity of society, and yet self-supporting.

Cheng Quan thought:

Restrain desire.

Keep the mind clear and bright.

Seek the Truth.

Attain the Way and awaken.

---

Cheng Quan came to see me again.

I asked him first, “How were the blessings in your dream?”

“Immortal Master, you knew it all?”

“I knew.”

Cheng Quan asked,

“Do you know why I have come?”

“I do not.”

“I wish to understand the riddle of humankind!”

“That question is far too vast.” I laughed aloud.

Cheng Quan did not laugh. “Immortal Master, you can tell me!” His eyes blazed as he fixed them upon me, his body utterly still.

I nodded. “I will tell you, but not now.”

“When?”

“Tonight,” I told him. “We will speak of it tonight!”

That night, Cheng Quan dreamed that I came to him.

But that “I” was not in any worldly form —

In the midst of empty space the sun and moon appeared, their light pouring upon a lotus in full bloom. The lotus radiated intense light, and upon it sat a yidam, a Buddha-Bodhisattva.

Around it were eight more lotus flowers, and upon each lotus appeared a bodhisattva, all facing one another. Each bodhisattva held an auspicious offering with which to make offering to the yidam, the Buddha-Bodhisattva.

The Eight Offering Bodhisattvas sang:


> Supreme and most honored Lian Sheng Buddha,  
> Like a bodhisattva come into the Saha World;  
> In one age there is but a single opening;  
> Most rare indeed, the manifestation of Tummo.  

They sang again:


> So hard to seek out, so hard to encounter;  
> Only those with karmic affinity are able to behold it.  
> Today the Buddha bestows upon you the Great Mandala;  
> Swiftly give rise to the great vow of great compassion.  

Cheng Quan was spellbound. Looking more closely at the countenance of the yidam, the Buddha-Bodhisattva, he found it was none other than the face of Lu Sheng-yen — exactly the same.

He was utterly astounded!

“Lu Sheng-yen the fortune-teller is actually a Buddha-Bodhisattva?”

He joined his palms, took refuge, and received empowerment.

Cheng Quan asked,

“The riddle of humankind?”

I replied,

“The Great Cycle of Rebirth.”

“And who am I?”

I brought out a chart of rebirths for Cheng Quan to see. The moment he looked, he was greatly shocked: in his former life Cheng Quan had been the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea (龙君).

His Queen had been a Dragon Maiden.

And there were many other details recorded there as well.…

So the matter of the “blessings in the dream” was not wholly illusion, but a fact of a former life. Cheng Quan wept.

I said, “Though you were the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, the gatherings and partings of a human life last but a hundred years. Why grieve over them like a child?”

Cheng Quan asked, “Why did I lose the body of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea?”

“Simply because the karmic affinity was exhausted!”

“How may I restore it now?”

“Practice the Dragon King Dharma according to my instruction.”

When Cheng Quan awoke, he considered the two dreams in detail, and his faith became deeply firm. There was no denying that both dreams were extraordinary. He began to take an interest in supernormal phenomena, holding that these were neither psychological nor sensory phenomena, but that genuine verification lay within them.

Cheng Quan wished to lift the veil of mystery from the Great Cycle of Rebirth, and his longing grew ever stronger. Many hidden truths lay within it. He treasured the two dreams quietly in his heart, guarding them like a secret, and set out upon his quest.

---

Cheng Quan came to see me a third time.

He sat before me without uttering a word.

Nor did I say anything. I simply took up a book and gave it to him. The title of the book was The Dragon King Sutra.

The moment he saw the title, he knelt and prostrated before me.

“What is it?” I asked.

“Immortal Master, you are a Buddha.”

I asked,

“Do you wish to learn?”

“I do.”

“Do you wish to return to your original station?”

“I do.”

“Good. Very good.”

So I said to Cheng Quan that the “Dragon King Dharma” is a very great Tantric Dharma. The Mahayana scriptures greatly praise the protective power of the Eight Legions — devas, nagas, yakshas, gandharvas, asuras, garudas, kinnaras, and mahoragas — for they all vowed before the Buddha to protect practitioners and the Buddhadharma.

Dragon Kings possess the five supernormal powers. They are Dragon Kings of great blessings, and they can also achieve the Tantric Dharma attainments of Pacification, Enrichment, Magnetization, and Subjugation.

As for blessings: in the heavens, the Heavenly Kings have the greatest blessings; upon the earth, the Mountain Kings have the greatest blessings; within the waters, the Dragon Kings have the greatest blessings.

As for cultivation, among the Thirty-five Buddhas there is one called Dragon Venerable King Buddha (Nagesvararaja), who was a Dragon King that cultivated and attained Buddhahood.

Moreover, the Dragon Maiden in the Dragon King Sutra also received the Buddha’s prediction before him that she would attain Buddhahood in the future.

That Dragon Kings attain Buddhahood was recorded in the scriptures long ago; therefore the merits and virtues of the Dragon Kings are exceedingly great.

According to the Buddhist scriptures, there are sixty thousand Dragon Kings in this world.

Shakyamuni Buddha once spoke of four inconceivables —

1. The inconceivability of worlds.

2. The inconceivability of sentient beings.

3. The inconceivability of dragons.

4. The inconceivability of Buddha lands.

I taught Cheng Quan.

The “Outer Dharma,” the “Inner Dharma,” the “Secret Dharma,” and the “Innermost Secret Dharma” — one by one I transmitted them to him.

Here the Outer Dharma refers to the ritual for practicing the Dragon King Dharma. The Inner Dharma refers to how one transforms oneself into the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea: the yidam enters me and I enter (入我我入). The Secret Dharma refers to how the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea cultivates to Buddhahood, that is, turning from dragon into Buddha. Finally, the Innermost Secret Dharma is the boundless light of the Dharma Realm filling the whole Dharma Realm, delivering sentient beings and bringing them to Buddhahood.

I composed a verse in praise of the Dragon King:


> The Dragon’s power is most mighty and fierce;  
> The Tathagata has ever praised it.  
> Entrusted with delivering sentient beings,  
> Its spiritual powers are supreme and unsurpassed.  

---

After I came to America, the Dragon King of the waters off Kemeite Island [Kismet?] once manifested: though the wind was still and the sea calm, the waves suddenly surged. This Dragon King led countless dragon sons and dragon grandsons in paying homage to me.

I have entered the Dragon Palace.

I have personally seen the form of the celestial dragons in the Dragon Pool; they differ somewhat from the dragon images of the human world.

I taught my disciples to offer Dragon King Treasure Vases, and when the Treasure Vases were offered, the shadow of a dragon appeared upon the mountainside.

I prayed for rain, and rain came.

The Dragon Rock of Seattle is renowned throughout the world.

Cheng Quan learned the Dragon King Dharma. Transforming karma into wisdom, his blessings too became great, and he has realized fusion into oneness with the Dragon King.
